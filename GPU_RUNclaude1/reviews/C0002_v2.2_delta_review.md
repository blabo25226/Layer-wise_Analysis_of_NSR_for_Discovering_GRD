## 1. Question 1 — does §19 fire on every fired P1, and is it a real substitute?

**It fires. It is not a substitute.** Both halves matter and they have different answers.

### 1.1 The firing is airtight — say so plainly

§19's trigger is "**either certified lower bound reported as prevalent**". The §13.2 branch name is
"`H0001-R` refuted — **certified search error prevalent**", so a fired cycle-level P1 is inside the
trigger by construction, not by interpretation. It is reinforced in three further places: §12.2
step 4 ("**§19 is now P1's only cross-check and may not be waived**"), §19 itself ("a fired P1 that
is not taken through §19 is a contract violation"), and **§24** ("§19's replication gate may not be
waived on a fired P1"). §19 also correctly rules the second context out as a replication factor, on
two independent grounds. **That part of the design is done properly and I have no finding on it.**

### 1.2 The substitute is broken — CRITICAL

§19's menu of admissible independent factors is:

> a fresh process and artifact path; a different `CONTROL_CELL_SEED`; **the other scoring context**;
> or an independent re-derivation of `lp_best` from a separately written scorer.

Taken one at a time, against P1:

| factor | does it perturb the cycle-level P1 value? |
|---|---|
| **the other scoring context** | **The same section, two paragraphs later, says it satisfies none of the four factors** — and for P1 gives a second reason (rule 0). The menu contains an option §19 itself declares invalid. |
| a different `CONTROL_CELL_SEED` | **No.** §10 (line 1651): `CONTROL_CELL_SEED` draws the **40-cell control set** and NC-CONDITION's per-cell partner. P1 is computed over **all 80 systems / 960 cells** (§22). Changing this seed re-runs the controls and leaves P1 **bit-identical**. |
| a fresh process and artifact path | **Effectively no.** The scoring pass is teacher-forced, fp32, on stored candidates, with no sampling; a fresh process reproduces the number up to GPU-kernel float nondeterminism. That is a reproducibility check, not a check of the quantity's validity. |
| an independently written scorer | **Yes.** This is the only factor on the menu that is a genuine cross-check of P1. |

§19 requires "**at least one**" factor. So a replication that changes only the process path, or only
`CONTROL_CELL_SEED`, formally discharges the gate the contract calls **P1's only cross-check** while
checking nothing about P1. Note the two neighbouring lists — §12.2 step 4 (line 1992) and §21 item 11
(line 2693) — both already omit "the other scoring context", which confirms its presence in §19 is an
**orphan from v1/v2.1**, not a considered choice.

**Third limb, and the one that makes this CRITICAL rather than MAJOR: §19 has no consequence.**
Its outcomes are `replicated` / `directionally replicated` / `failed replication` / `inconclusive`,
"recorded separately under `GPU_RUNclaude1/replications/`". **Nothing anywhere in the contract states
what a `failed replication` does to a fired P1 verdict.** §13 has no branch for it; §23/§24 do not
mention it. So the mechanism on which §12.2 step 4 rests its entire honesty argument produces a label
with no binding effect on the conclusion it exists to police. A rule 2 conjunction would have
**prevented** the verdict from forming; §19 only asks that a formed verdict be revisited, and then
does not say what happens when it is not confirmed.

**Required change (no threshold moves):**
1. Delete "the other scoring context" from §19's factor menu.
2. State that **for a fired P1**, the replication must use a factor that actually perturbs P1 — the
   independently-written scorer, or an independently written `C_gen` forward-map reconstruction.
   Name `CONTROL_CELL_SEED` and the fresh process path as insufficient **for P1**, with the reason.
3. Preregister the consequence of each §19 outcome for the P1 cycle verdict, including
   `failed replication` and `inconclusive`. A gate with no stated consequence is not a gate.

### 1.3 The honest position the supervisor asked about

The supervisor asked whether the honest position is that **P1's evidentiary standard dropped and the
contract should say so more loudly**. My answer: **§12.2 step 4 already says it about as loudly as
prose can** — "P1 carries a single-context read", "it has no internal cross-check", "that incidental
conservatism is gone, and this contract says so rather than implying the protection survives in
another form". That paragraph is exemplary and should not be softened.

What is missing is not volume but **enforceability**. The sentence "§19 is now P1's only cross-check"

(b) has no consequence when it fails. Repair §19 and the loudness is already adequate; leave §19 as
written and the loudness becomes a liability, because the contract will have declared a protection it
does not deliver — which is worse than declaring none.

## 2. Question 2 — is the commensurability argument right? Attacking it

**The supervisor's conclusion is right. Rule 0, as frozen in v2.2, is not the supervisor's argument,
and as written it is false and symmetric.** This is MAJOR-1.

### 2.1 What rule 0 says

| certificate | rule 0's stated ground |
|---|---|
| P2 | commensurable, because `me_best` "certifies that the truth is not the model's argmax **under the conditioning it was scored in**. That proposition is the same proposition in both contexts; only the conditioning differs." |
| P1 | not commensurable, because `C_raw`'s P1 "is **not a search-error claim**"; "**these are two different quantities**". |

### 2.2 Why the P2 row is false

"A proposition indexed to a conditioning is the same proposition when the conditioning differs" is
self-contradicting. `C_gen` and `C_raw` induce **different conditional distributions** over token
sequences. "The truth is not the argmax of `P(· | x_scaled,permuted)`" and "the truth is not the
argmax of `P(· | x_raw)`" are **logically independent** statements: a model can place the truth at
the top under one conditioning and not the other. They are no more "the same proposition" than the
two P1 readings are.

### 2.3 Why that breaks the asymmetry, and why it is dangerous

The P1 row's ground — "these are two different quantities" — applies **verbatim** to the P2 row.
Rule 0 therefore proves the same thing about both rows and **cannot support the asymmetry it
asserts**. Run it consistently and you get one of two conclusions, both wrong:

- take the P2 row's standard ⇒ P1's two readings are also "the same proposition scored under
  different conditionings" ⇒ **rule 2 should have been kept for P1**, and v2.2 removed a real
  control; or
- take the P1 row's standard ⇒ P2's two readings are also different quantities ⇒ **rule 2 should be
  dropped for P2 as well.**

The second is the live hazard, because rule 0's language is now **frozen in §13.4, §22, §24, §12.2
step 3, §8.7 and §26.2/§26.5**. A later cycle citing rule 0 could argue, from the contract's own
text, that P2's contexts are equally non-commensurable and drop the cross-context requirement — which
would remove the last full conjunction protecting **the campaign's preferred direction**. That is
exactly the failure the direction check of §12.2 step 6 was designed to prevent, re-entering through
the premise rather than through the rule.

### 2.4 The correct ground is available, is the supervisor's own, and reaches the same conclusion

`C0002_supervisor_decisions.md` 追補 states it: **`me_best` は探索の意味論を帯びておらず** — Certificate N
carries no search semantics. That is the real asymmetry, and it is not about commensurability at all:

- **Certificate S is indexed to a historical event.** It asserts that *the decode that ran* failed to
  return the argmax. There was exactly **one** decode, under generation conditioning. A
  Certificate-S-shaped number computed under a conditioning no decode ever used is a statement about
  a search that never happened — which is precisely what §13.2 branch 1 and §8.7 already encode.
- **Certificate N is a property of the model.** It asserts that the model does not rank the truth
  first. Both conditionings are legitimate probes of that, and **neither is privileged** — so a
  Certificate-N conclusion that holds under only one conditioning is conditioning-dependent and
  should be reported as such. That is a robustness demand, and it survives intact without any claim
  that the two readings are "the same proposition".

**So: I could not break the conclusion, and the supervisor did not cause a lost control.** Rule 2 was
genuinely incoherent for P1, and P2's retention is genuinely justified — on the supervisor's ground,
not on the methodologist's paraphrase of it.

**Required change (no threshold moves):** rewrite rule 0 to run on **search semantics and decode
indexing**, not on commensurability. Replace the P2 row's justification with "neither conditioning is
privileged for a claim about the model's preference ordering, so a conclusion holding in only one is
conditioning-dependent". Replace "the two values are not the same quantity" in §8.7 and §7.3.2
condition 4 with "the `C_raw` value is not a claim about the decode that produced the candidate set".
The prohibition on reading `C_raw`'s P1 as confirming **or disconfirming** `C_gen`'s survives
unchanged under the corrected ground — indeed more cleanly, because it no longer depends on a false
premise about propositional identity.

## 4. Question 4 — are §26.6's answers sufficient? Two are; one is false

§26.6 anticipates: *"scoping rule 2 to P2 promotes `C_gen` for P1, and it happens to help the branch

**Answer 1 — "it is not a promotion." SUFFICIENT, and verifiable.** `C_gen` has been the only context

dating to v1. I checked: v1 §7.1 already restricted the `C_raw` `G > B` reading. §24 freezes the
prohibition on describing it as a promotion. This answer holds.

**Answer 3 — "the direction is against the campaign's preference." SUFFICIENT for the half it
addresses**, per §3 above. It answers the *E2-preference* half of the objection. It does **not**
answer the *newsworthiness* half, which is the half §26.6 itself raised.

**Answer 4 — "what a reviewer should still attack."** Good faith, correctly aimed, and it is the
finding of §1 above. No complaint.

**Answer 2 — "no realized value is known." FALSE AS STATED. This is MAJOR-2.**

§26.6 answer 2 reads: *"§0.4 still forbids `lp_best` under `C_gen`, so **no P1 value in either
context has been computed or seen by anyone**. The change cannot have been fitted to an outcome
because there is no outcome."*

The contract contradicts this twice, in its own text:

- **§0.3** discloses that `lp_gt`, `lp_best` and **`sb_best`** were computed and printed on **8 of
  960 cells under `C_raw`**, and records `sb_best = 0` on **all eight**.
- **§0.3 item 2 (line 215)** states, as the contract's own adopted position: *"with `lp_gt − lp_best`
  gaps of 100–500 nats, so `sb_best` is very likely to agree across contexts on those 8 cells"*, and
  then, in a passage that may not be softened: *"**A co-primary endpoint's own proxy was observed,
  before freeze, on 8 of 960 cells, in the direction the campaign prefers.** This is prior
  information about a co-primary context. It is **not** contained."*

So partial, directional outcome information about `C_raw`'s P1 **does** exist, and by the contract's
own reasoning it is informative about `C_gen`'s. Its direction is the one in which the removed
cross-context conjunction would have **bound against** a fired `C_gen` P1: eight observed cells all
at `sb_best = 0` is the `C_raw` P1 running low, i.e. exactly the configuration in which requiring
agreement would have killed a fired `C_gen` P1. **Answer 2 therefore denies, in §26, prior
information that §0.3 discloses and refuses to call contained** — and it does so in the one place
where the objection is about outcome-fitting.

This does **not** mean the change was outcome-fitted. Answer 1 is a genuine and dated defence, and 8
of 960 cells is thin. But the correct form of answer 2 is disclosure, not denial, and §0.3 already
shows the contract knows how to write it.

**Required change (no threshold moves):** replace §26.6 answer 2 with the §0.3-consistent statement —
no **aggregate** P1 value in either context has been computed; **8 of 960 cells were observed under
`C_raw`, all at `sb_best = 0`**, which is the direction in which the removed conjunction would have
bound; the change is defended by answer 1's dating, not by an absence of outcome information. And add
to answer 3 the explicit concession that the direction check covers the E2-preference objection and
**not** the newsworthiness objection, which answer 1 must carry alone.

## 8. Verdict

contract had already barred from making the claim was incoherent, the supervisor identified it on a
ground internal to the contract and visible without data, and the fix was implemented with unusual
thoroughness — §8.7, §16.2, §22 and §24 were all carried through, and §12.2 step 4 concedes the cost

contains a factor it elsewhere declares invalid, two of the remaining three are inert with respect to
P1's value, and no outcome of the gate has any stated consequence for the verdict it exists to
police. Fix §19 first — it is the single change on which the honesty of the whole delta depends.
Then repair rule 0's premise, §26.6 answer 2, and the two orphans.

None of the seven changes requires moving a threshold, and none requires recomputing anything. This
is a text pass, and after it I would not object to freezing.
